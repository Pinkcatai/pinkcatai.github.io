# Market Research Report: Smart Glasses (AI / AR glasses) in Germany

*Prepared: 18 November 2026. Sources are mostly from 2025-2026. Figures marked **estimate** come from third-party forecasts or my own reasoning and should not be treated as measured data. "No public data found" means I searched and found no reliable source.*

---

## 1. Market trends summary

### 1.1 Global and European momentum
- **Shipments are growing fast.** Counterpoint Research reports global smart-glasses shipments up **110% YoY in H1 2025** and **139% YoY in H2 2025**. Counterpoint counts sell-in to retailers, not sales to end customers, and says the growth rates are partly flattered by a low starting base. AI glasses (camera + audio + assistant, no display) made up **88%** of H2 2025 shipments. Display glasses are tracked separately.
  - Sources: https://www.counterpointresearch.com/en/insights/Global-Smart-Glasses-Shipments-Grew-139-Percent-YoY-in-H2-2025 ; https://heise.de/-11197067 (heise, 3 Mar 2026)
- **Western Europe is now a core region.** It took **30%** of global smart-glasses shipments in H2 2025, behind North America (37%) and well ahead of China (6%). No Germany-only shipment figure is published. (Same sources.)
- **Display/AR glasses are growing too.** Counterpoint reports global AR smart-glasses shipments **+148% YoY in H2 2025**, with waveguide-based devices up more than 600%. Rokid says Counterpoint ranked it **#1 in AI glasses with a display (41% share, H1 2026)**. That is a vendor-reported claim.
  - https://counterpointresearch.com/de/insights/Global-AR-Smart-Glasses-Shipments-Grow-148-Percent-YoY-in-H2-2025-Waveguide-based-Devices-Surge-Over-600-Percent ; https://mixed-news.com/en/?p=35020
- **IDC:** global XR device shipments grew **+44.4% in 2025**, driven mostly by smart glasses. IDC forecasts **+33.5% in 2026** and a **26.5% CAGR for 2026-2030**. These figures cover all XR worldwide. https://itbrief.co.uk/story/smart-glasses-drive-44-growth-in-global-xr-shipments
- **The Meta / EssilorLuxottica engine is decelerating.** The two companies sold more than **7 million** smart glasses in 2025. AI-glasses sales nearly doubled YoY in Q2 2026. In Q3 2026, EssilorLuxottica's wearables revenue (Meta glasses plus Nuance Audio hearing glasses) grew **+20% to about €486m**, a sharp slowdown from Q2. EssilorLuxottica does not split out Europe or Germany for wearables.
  - https://mixed-news.com/en/essilorluxottica-q3-2026-wearables-revenue-up-20-percent/ ; https://www.visionmonday.com/eyecare/article/ai-smart-glasses-and-myopia-management-portfolio-drive-q2-and-first-half-revenue-growth-for-essilorluxottica/

### 1.2 Germany / DACH market size
- **No official or measured German market-size figure was found.** The available numbers come from paid forecast reports and vary widely depending on how each firm defines the category:
  - MarketsandMarkets (smart glasses, Germany): **about USD 68m in 2024, rising to about USD 299m by 2029 (about 28% CAGR). This is an estimate.** https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/Market-Reports/geography/smart-glasses-market/Germany
  - MarketsandMarkets (AR glass, Germany): **about USD 75m in 2025, rising to about USD 801m by 2030 (about 61% CAGR). This is an estimate.** https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/Market-Reports/geography/ar-glass-market/Germany
  - Bonafide Research (Germany): **>7.7% CAGR for 2025-2030. This is an estimate, and no value was disclosed publicly.** https://www.bonafideresearch.com/product/6506491003/germany-smart-glasses-market
- **My sanity check (estimate):** Western Europe took about 30% of global shipments, and Meta/EssilorLuxottica alone sold more than 7m units in 2025. That implies Western Europe sell-in of roughly 1.5-2.5m units in 2025. Germany's share of Western European consumer electronics is typically about 20-25%, so German volume would be roughly **0.3-0.6m units in 2025**. This is an unverified back-of-envelope estimate. Fielmann's comments (below) suggest German demand is *below* the European average, so the lower end is more plausible.

### 1.3 Demand signals and attitudes in Germany
- **Bitkom survey (Aug 2026; n=1,164, age 16+, fieldwork weeks 20-24 of 2026):**
  - **15%** have already used smart glasses, in daily life or on a trial basis.
  - **28%** would like to; together that is **43% interested**.
  - **52%** say wearing them is out of the question.
  - **76%** do not want to be filmed or photographed by others' smart glasses.
  - **62%** would not let such wearers into their home.
  - **82%** want a clear recording indicator.
  - Bitkom opposes blanket bans and calls for clear rules plus privacy-by-design.
  - https://www.bitkom.org/Presse/Presseinformation/An-Smart-Glasses-scheiden-sich-die-Geister ; https://www.zdfheute.de/panorama/smartglasses-kamera-ueberwachung-umfrage-100.html
- **Germany's largest optician is cautious.** Fielmann CEO Marc Fielmann said in Sep 2026 that smart glasses will not replace smartphones in the next 2-3 years and are a niche, "closer to sports sunglasses than AirPods". Fielmann sells them in only about **200 of about 1,300 stores**. Demand is strongest in big cities, Spain and the USA, and **Germany is not among the high-demand markets**. Fielmann would only roll out to all stores if demand were "bombastic". https://www.drohnen.de/89054/fielmann-smart-glasses/ ; https://www.drweb.de/smartglasses-fielmann-nische-filialen/
- **Product and price trends:**
  - The category is moving down-market. In June 2026 Meta launched non-Ray-Ban "Meta Glasses" from USD 299, about €200 below Ray-Ban Meta Gen 2. Xiaomi audio glasses sell for about €100.
  - It is also moving up into display glasses: Meta Ray-Ban Display at €899 in Germany from 13 Oct 2026, Rokid Glasses at €699, Even G2 at €699.
  - Google/Samsung Android XR glasses are launching in the US and Korea first, with no confirmed EU date.
  - Sources: https://t3n.de/news/meta-verzichtet-auf-ray-ban-logo-und-macht-seine-smarten-brillen-200-euro-guenstiger-1749113/ ; https://mixed-news.com/en/?p=34442 ; https://www.googlewatchblog.de/2026/06/android-xr-googles-erste-smart-glasses-starten-bald-xreal-aura-brillen-kommen-auch-nach-deutschland/

### 1.4 Policy direction
- **The regulatory climate is getting stricter in Germany, and it is focused on camera glasses.**
  - Hamburg's data protection commissioner (HmbBfDI, Thomas Fuchs) said Meta glasses can hardly be operated in public in a GDPR-compliant way. He considers a ban under the TDDDG's prohibition of disguised recording devices conceivable.
  - The Bundesnetzagentur currently does **not** prohibit possession or sale as long as recording is clearly signalled (e.g. by an LED). It has no formal proceeding open.
  - NGO HateAid filed a criminal complaint (under § 27 TKG) against Meta, Ray-Ban, Oakley, and retailers **Fielmann, Apollo-Optik, Mister Spex and MediaMarkt**. It is still pending.
  - The Federal Ministry of Justice drafted a law in April 2026 on intimate-sphere image violations.
  - Sources: https://netzpolitik.org/2026/heimliches-filmen-kann-man-ueberwachungsbrillen-verbieten/ (19 Aug 2026) ; https://borncity.com/news/meta-datenbrillen-hamburgischer-datenschutzbeauftragter-erklaert-sie-fuer-rechtswidrig/
- **Industrial policy is supportive but there is no dedicated subsidy.** No German subsidy specifically for smart glasses was found. Enterprise deployments fall under general Industrie 4.0 and digitalisation funding (e.g. the "Digital Jetzt" type SME programmes). *I did not verify that any of these programmes are currently open.*

## 3. Target customer profiles

### 3.1 Consumer segments
1. **Urban early adopters and creators (estimate: ages 18-40, big cities)**
   - What they want: hands-free POV photo/video, music and calls, AI Q&A, live translation.
   - Purchase drivers: design and brand (Ray-Ban), price under €500, Instagram/WhatsApp integration.
   - Fielmann confirms demand is concentrated in large cities.
2. **Glasses wearers who want "one pair for everything"**
   - The prescription-lens route through opticians is key, with both Ray-Ban Meta Optics and Even G2 targeting it.
   - Pain points: fitting, price of lenses (an extra €169 or more for Even G2), and comfort for all-day wear.
3. **Sports and outdoor users** (Oakley Meta, cycling, running). Purchase drivers: open-ear audio, durability, battery life.
4. **Travellers and language users**: live translation is a headline feature for Rokid, Even and Meta.
5. **Accessibility users** (blind or low-vision, hard-of-hearing): scene description, captions. This is a high-value niche with potential for health-insurer or aid funding (*not verified*). https://borncity.com/news/meta-setzt-auf-smarte-brillen-ki-hilfe-fuer-blinde-und-gehoerlose/
6. **Media and productivity users**: Xreal/Viture buyers who want a big virtual screen for trains, flights and laptops.

**What holds consumers back:**
- **Privacy and social acceptance.** 76% don't want to be filmed and 52% reject wearing smart glasses (Bitkom 2026).
- Being associated with Meta/data concerns.
- Price, battery life, and a limited feature set in the EU.

### 3.2 Enterprise segments
1. **Manufacturing / Industrie 4.0** (automotive, machinery, the Mittelstand): work instructions, quality inspection, remote expert support.
2. **Logistics / warehousing** (3PLs, retail DCs): pick-by-vision, hands-free scanning.
3. **Field service / maintenance** (energy, elevators, HVAC, telecoms): remote assist to cut technician travel and to cope with the shortage of skilled workers (Fachkräftemangel).
4. **Healthcare**: documentation, tele-consultation, training. Strict data protection applies; the Thuringian DPA has published guidance on smart glasses in hospitals (https://tlfdi.de/fileadmin/tlfdi/info/Veroeffentlichungen_nach_dem_ThuerTG/5/Smart_Classes_im_Krankenhaus_.pdf). Clinical use may trigger EU MDR medical-device rules (*general regulatory knowledge, verify per use case*).

**Enterprise buyer criteria:**
- Proven ROI (errors, travel, training time)
- Ruggedness and safety (ATEX, IP ratings)
- Integration with SAP/MES/WMS
- On-prem or EU data hosting
- A works-council-compatible design (see 4.1)
- Long-term support, which matters given HoloLens' end of life

The decision-makers are operations/plant management, IT/OT security, the data protection officer (DSB) and the works council (Betriebsrat).

## 4. Barriers and opportunities for market entry

### 4.1 Barriers
1. **GDPR / DSGVO and German privacy enforcement**
   - Recording identifiable people is processing personal data. HmbBfDI argues that an LED is insufficient, works only at short range and in good light, and does not equal consent.
   - For AI features such as model training, HmbBfDI sees **joint controllership (Art. 26 GDPR)** between the wearer and the manufacturer.
   - Expect scrutiny of cloud processing, AI training on EU user data, and US data transfers.
   - https://borncity.com/news/meta-datenbrillen-hamburgischer-datenschutzbeauftragter-erklaert-sie-fuer-rechtswidrig/ ; https://borncity.com/news/meta-ki-brille-signal-led-kann-bei-aufnahmen-schwach-leuchten-oder-ausbleiben/
2. **TDDDG / TKG "disguised recording device" risk**
   - This is a Germany-specific rule: devices that disguise themselves as another object and covertly record images may be banned from possession and sale.
   - The Bundesnetzagentur currently tolerates devices with a clear recording signal.
   - The HateAid criminal complaint (§ 27 TKG) is pending against manufacturers **and retailers**, which may make retailers more cautious. https://netzpolitik.org/2026/heimliches-filmen-kann-man-ueberwachungsbrillen-verbieten/
3. **Right to one's own image (Recht am eigenen Bild, §§ 22-23 KUG) and personality rights**
   - Filming in public is not generally forbidden, but **publishing or distributing** images of identifiable people without consent usually is.
   - Recording the *non-public spoken word* is a criminal offence (§ 201 StGB).
   - § 201a StGB protects the most private sphere of life.
   - Venues (cinemas, gyms, saunas, hospitals, workplaces) increasingly ban camera glasses. *The legal summary is general knowledge; get German counsel.* https://pasqualepillitteri.it/de/news/12112/meta-smarte-brille-datenschutz
4. **EU AI Act** (*summary of the Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 timeline; verify against the current text and the "Digital Omnibus" amendments*)
   - **Prohibited practices (since 2 Feb 2025):**
     - untargeted scraping of facial images
     - **emotion recognition in the workplace and in education**, which is relevant for enterprise glasses
     - biometric categorisation by sensitive traits
     - real-time remote biometric identification in public for law enforcement, with narrow exceptions
   - **Face-recognition features** on consumer glasses would face a very high legal risk under the AI Act and GDPR Art. 9. Meta reportedly considered adding them (NYT, Feb 2026, per gdprlocal).
   - **Obligations for general-purpose AI models apply from Aug 2025.** If you use third-party LLMs, check the provider's compliance.
   - **Transparency duties (Art. 50) and high-risk rules** phase in from 2026-2027 and may be delayed by the Omnibus.
   - https://gdprlocal.com/smart-glasses/ ; https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/
5. **CE marking and product rules**
   - **RED 2014/53/EU** (radio: Bluetooth and Wi-Fi) plus EMC and LVD/safety apply.
   - **From 1 Aug 2025**, RED Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/30 requires cybersecurity, privacy and fraud protection (Art. 3(3)(d)(e)(f)), with presumption of conformity via the **EN 18031-1/-2/-3** standards, harmonised with restrictions.
     - Internet-connected devices that process personal data, and wearables, are in scope. *Confirm scope per device with a test lab.*
     - The **Cyber Resilience Act** takes over these requirements from **11 Dec 2027**.
     - https://nemko.com/blog/cybersecurity-requirements-ce-marking-postponed-till-1-august-2025?hsLang=en ; https://vde.com/tic-en/news/2025/01-cyber-security-red
   - Other rules (*general knowledge*):
     - The **EU Battery Regulation (EU) 2023/1542**: from Feb 2027 portable batteries must be removable or replaceable by the end user, with some exceptions. This is reportedly one reason for the delayed EU launch of Meta Ray-Ban Display.
     - **German ElektroG/WEEE** registration with stiftung ear, **VerpackG** packaging registration, and **BattDG** battery registration.
     - The **GPSR** product-safety regulation, which applies since Dec 2024.
     - **USB-C common charger** rules, if applicable.
     - Laser/LED eye-safety standards for display optics.
   - Prescription inserts may involve medical-device or optician rules.
6. **Works councils / co-determination (enterprise)**
   - Under § 87(1) No. 6 BetrVG, any technical system that *can* monitor behaviour or performance needs a works agreement (Betriebsvereinbarung).
   - The BAG ruled this applies even to headset systems that do not record (1 ABR 16/23).
   - Rolling out without an agreement risks injunctions. This adds months to enterprise sales cycles.
   - https://www.kliemt.blog/2026/09/21/wenn-die-brille-zur-kamera-wird-smart-glasses-am-arbeitsplatz/ ; https://www.betriebsrat.de/news/datenschutz/smart-glasses-am-arbeitsplatz-helfer-oder-kontrollinstrument-5008778
7. **Localisation**
   - Users expect high-quality German voice assistant, UI, support and documentation.
   - Meta AI's German voice support only arrived in **Apr 2025**, with some features lagging the US. https://heise.de/-10360435
8. **Channel concentration and incumbents' lock-in**
   - EssilorLuxottica owns Ray-Ban/Oakley, makes Meta's glasses and controls large optical retail. Fielmann (the German market leader in optics) and Apollo already stock Meta.
   - MediaMarkt/Saturn (Ceconomy) dominate consumer electronics retail and also sell Meta. Shelf space and optician fitting capacity for new brands are limited.
   - Some retailers are cautious because of the HateAid complaint.

### 4.2 Opportunities
1. **Privacy-first differentiation** (the strongest Germany-specific angle)
   - Camera-free display glasses (like Even G2) and audio glasses avoid the TDDDG/KUG debate.
   - For camera models, design in clearly visible indicators that cannot be disabled (82% demand this), on-device processing, EU hosting, no AI training on bystander data, and a hardware camera shutter.
   - This is exactly the "technical solution" Bitkom calls for.
2. **Non-Meta brand positioning**: a share of German consumers distrust Meta. A European or "data-sovereign" brand story could resonate. *This is inference: no survey splitting trust by brand was found.*
3. **Opticians as a channel**
   - Fielmann positions itself as a sales, fitting and service partner for *any* manufacturer and stocks only about 15% of stores so far, so there is room for more brands.
   - Independent opticians (about 12,000 businesses in Germany, *estimate, not verified in this research*), Apollo, and Mister Spex (online) are alternatives.
   - Prescription integration is a must-have.
4. **Consumer electronics retail and online**: MediaMarkt/Saturn and Amazon.de. Rokid entered Germany via its own store plus Amazon with a launch event in Munich, which is a low-cost playbook to copy.
5. **Enterprise replacement cycle**
   - HoloLens 2 has reached end of life (security updates end Dec 2027; Dynamics 365 Remote Assist/Guides reportedly end Dec 2026).
   - Combined with the shortage of skilled workers, this creates demand in German automotive, machinery and logistics for lighter AI-assisted glasses.
   - Partnering with TeamViewer Frontline or SAP-integrated ISVs reduces integration friction.
6. **Accessibility and healthcare niches**: assistance for visually or hearing-impaired users, and clinical documentation. Higher willingness to pay, with possible reimbursement routes (*to be verified*).
7. **Price gap**: between budget audio glasses (about €100) and Ray-Ban Meta Gen 2 (about €420-500), and between AI glasses and display glasses (€699-899), there is room for a **€300-450 AI glasses** or a **€500-600 display glasses** with strong German-language AI.
8. **Timing window**: Google/Samsung Android XR glasses have no EU launch date yet, and Apple is expected in 2027. **2026-H1 2027** is a window before platform giants arrive in Germany.
