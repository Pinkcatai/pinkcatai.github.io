# Strategy Positioning Document: Smart Glasses for Germany (working name: KLAR)

*Based solely on `01-research.md` (18 Nov 2026). Section references such as [R 4.2] point to that report. Where the report gives no evidence for a decision, this document says so instead of filling the gap.*

**Working name: KLAR.** It is German for "clear". The name points to clear vision, clear German-language AI and clear data practices, which is the brand promise in one word. Trademark availability has not been checked: **insufficient information, further research recommended.**

**Core product decision:** KLAR is **camera-free display glasses** (HUD with captions, translation, navigation and notifications, plus a German-language AI assistant). They are **prescription-ready through opticians** and target the **€500-600** price gap [R 4.2.7].

Why camera-free rather than a cheaper camera AI glasses product:
- The German barriers are almost entirely about cameras. 76% of Germans do not want to be filmed, 62% would not let wearers into their home [R 1.3], the Hamburg DPA questions GDPR compliance of camera glasses, and there are the TDDDG ban risk and the pending HateAid complaint [R 1.4, 4.1].
- Meta holds 82% global share in camera AI glasses [R 2.1] and is already pushing prices down to about US$299 [R 1.3]. Competing head-on there means fighting the leader on its home turf.

## 1. Product positioning

> **KLAR is the camera-free smart glasses made for Germany: for people who wear glasses and want live captions, translation and a German-speaking AI assistant in front of their eyes, without the camera that makes everyone around them uncomfortable, at a price below every display rival.**

Breaking the statement down:

| Element | Content | Evidence in report |
|---|---|---|
| Who we are | Privacy-first display smart glasses built for the German market | Privacy-first is "the strongest Germany-specific angle" [R 4.2.1] |
| For whom | Glasses wearers, primarily in large cities, who also travel or work across languages | Segments 2 and 4 [R 3.1]; demand concentrated in big cities [R 1.3] |
| What problem | They want hands-free information (captions, translation, navigation, AI answers) in their own prescription pair, but camera glasses are socially and legally contested in Germany | Bitkom figures, the legal barriers [R 1.3, 4.1] |
| How we differ | No camera, a display, German-native AI with EU data hosting, prescription via opticians, €500-600 | Ray-Ban Meta has a camera and no display. Ray-Ban Display costs €899 with no prescription in DE. Even G2 costs €699 + €169 [R 2.1, 2.2] |

"Below every display rival" refers to the display glasses listed in the report: Meta Ray-Ban Display €899, Rokid Glasses from €699, Even G2 €699 [R 2.2]. It assumes the €500-600 target is achievable (see 3.3).

## 2. Target segment

### Primary segment: glasses wearers who want "one pair for everything" [R 3.1, segment 2]

Why first:
1. **Large addressable base.** Meta's Optics line targets "the roughly 2/3 of adults who wear glasses" [R 2.1]. A camera-free display product does not need users to adopt a new habit. It replaces glasses they already wear every day.
2. **The channel already exists and has room for more brands.** Fielmann stocks smart glasses in only about 200 of about 1,300 stores and positions itself as a partner for any manufacturer [R 1.3, 4.2.3]. Apollo, Mister Spex and independent opticians are alternatives.
3. **Their pain points are ones we can address.** The report lists fitting, lens price (an extra €169+ for Even G2) and all-day comfort [R 3.1]. KLAR can compete on total price with lenses included.
4. **Meta's weak spot.** Meta Ray-Ban Display has no prescription lenses yet in Germany [R 2.2]. Ray-Ban Meta Optics has a camera and no display, and its German availability is unconfirmed [R 2.1].

### Secondary segment: travellers and language users [R 3.1, segment 4]

Why second:
1. **Live translation is a headline feature** across Rokid, Even and Meta [R 3.1]. On a HUD, translated subtitles work without the user filming anyone, which fits our camera-free design.
2. **They overlap strongly with segment 1** (urban, glasses-wearing professionals), so the same optician channel and the same messaging reach both. No separate channel is needed. The size of this overlap is not measured in the report.

### Segments deliberately deprioritised

| Segment | Why not now |
|---|---|
| Urban creators / POV video [R 3.1, segment 1] | Their core job (photo/video) needs a camera. Ray-Ban Meta dominates on brand and design. We concede this segment. |
| Sports / outdoor [R 3.1, segment 3] | Oakley Meta is established, and the report gives no German demand data for display glasses in sport. |
| Media / productivity (Xreal, Viture) [R 3.1, segment 6] | A different job-to-be-done (tethered big screen) needing different optics. |
| Accessibility (hard-of-hearing captions) [R 3.1, segment 5] | **This is a strong product fit for a captioning HUD, and a Phase 3 option.** Reimbursement or aid funding is unverified: **insufficient information, further research recommended** before committing resources. |
| Enterprise (Industrie 4.0, logistics, field service) [R 3.2] | The HoloLens 2 end of life creates a real opening [R 4.2.5]. However, buyers need ruggedness (ATEX/IP), SAP/MES/WMS integration and works-council agreements, and sales cycles are months long [R 3.2, 4.1.6]. A consumer HUD product is not built for that. Revisit as a separate product line. Whether our company has enterprise capabilities is **insufficient information, further research recommended.** |

**Sizing caveat:** The report has no Germany-specific unit data and no figures on how many glasses wearers are interested in smart glasses [R 5, data gaps]. The only volume figure is an unverified estimate of 0.3-0.6m units in 2025, probably at the lower end [R 1.2]. The segment size in units or euros is therefore **insufficient information, further research recommended.** Before committing to volumes, we need a quantitative survey of German glasses wearers covering interest, willingness to pay and acceptance of camera-free glasses.

## 3. Differentiation strategy

### 3.1 Versus Ray-Ban Meta (category leader, 82% global share)

| Dimension | Ray-Ban Meta Gen 2 / Optics | KLAR | Our message |
|---|---|---|---|
| Camera | Yes. The main target of German privacy criticism, the Hamburg DPA statement and the HateAid complaint [R 2.1, 1.4] | **None.** It cannot take photos or video, so the camera-specific TDDDG "disguised recording device" and KUG image-rights debate does not apply [R 4.1.2, 4.1.3]. Note: microphones for captions and translation still process speech, so audio must be processed without storage (see 3.3) | "Nobody around you has to wonder whether they are being filmed." |
| Display | None (only Meta Ray-Ban Display at €899) | HUD for captions, translation, navigation | "See the answer instead of hearing it." |
| Social acceptance | 52% reject wearing them. Venues (cinemas, gyms, saunas, hospitals, workplaces) increasingly ban camera glasses [R 1.3, 4.1.3] | Not affected by camera-glasses bans. Whether venues will distinguish camera-free glasses in practice is **insufficient information, further research recommended** | "Wear them wherever you wear normal glasses." |
| Data / trust | Meta brand. Some consumers distrust it (inference) [R 4.2.2]. Joint-controller concerns over AI training [R 4.1.1] | EU hosting, no training on user data, on-device processing where possible [R 4.2.1] | "Your data stays in the EU and is never used for training." |
| German-language AI | Meta AI in German only since Apr 2025, with some features lagging or limited in the EU [R 2.1, 4.1.7] | German-first assistant, UI, support and documentation | "Built for German, not translated into it." |
| Prescription | Optics line from €469, German availability unconfirmed. Display version has no prescription in DE [R 2.1, 2.2] | Prescription as a core use case via opticians | "Your next pair of glasses, not an extra gadget." |

**Where we will not compete with Ray-Ban Meta:** brand and fashion appeal, photo/video, Instagram/WhatsApp integration, and price points below €300. Ray-Ban Meta is the right product for creators, and we say so implicitly by not targeting them.

**Ray-Ban Meta's likely response (inference):** Meta can lower prices (it already has with Meta Glasses at about US$299) and could add prescription to the Display model in Germany. It is unlikely to remove the camera from its core line, because photo/video is that line's main use case. Our defensible differentiator is therefore the **camera-free plus German-trust** combination, not price alone.

### 3.3 Non-negotiable product requirements derived from the differentiation

These requirements support the positioning. If we cannot deliver one of them, the positioning does not hold.

1. **No camera hardware at all.** Not a shutter and not a disabled sensor. This is the whole basis of the TDDDG/KUG argument.
2. **Microphone privacy by design:** captions and translation processed transiently, with no audio stored by default and a visible indicator when the microphone is active. Recording the non-public spoken word is a criminal offence (§ 201 StGB) [R 4.1.3], so the product must not become an audio recorder. The exact legal requirements are **insufficient information, further research recommended** (German counsel).
3. **Prescription lens integration** through optician fitting.
4. **German-language AI at launch quality**, including the captioning and translation flows.
5. **EU data hosting, no training on user data, and published data practices.** Third-party LLM providers must be checked for GPAI compliance under the AI Act [R 4.1.4].
6. **No face recognition and no emotion recognition**, given AI Act and GDPR Art. 9 risks [R 4.1.4].
7. **Full EU compliance before launch:** RED including the EN 18031 cybersecurity requirements, EMC/LVD, GPSR, ElektroG/WEEE, VerpackG and BattDG registration, and eye-safety standards for the display [R 4.1.5].
8. **Battery design compatible with the EU Battery Regulation from Feb 2027** (user-removable or replaceable, or a valid exemption). The report cites this as a reported reason for Meta Ray-Ban Display's delayed EU launch [R 4.1.5]. **Whether glasses qualify for an exemption is insufficient information, further research recommended** (get a legal and test-lab opinion).

Whether our current hardware, AI stack and cost structure can deliver these requirements at €500-600 is not covered by the report: **insufficient information, further research recommended.** It is the single most important internal question before go/no-go.

## 4. Go-to-market plan (GTM)

### 4.1 Channel strategy

| Priority | Channel | Role | Rationale [R] |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Independent opticians and smaller optical chains in large cities** | Fitting, prescription, trial and sale | Prescription integration is a must-have, and demand is concentrated in big cities [R 4.2.3, 1.3]. Independents are a route around the limited shelf space and fitting capacity at the large chains [R 4.1.8]. The number of independent opticians (about 12,000) is unverified. |
| 2 | **Own D2C web shop + Amazon.de** | Non-prescription sales, reach, reviews | Rokid's low-cost playbook: own store plus Amazon plus a launch event in Munich [R 4.2.4] |
| 3 | **Mister Spex (online optician)** | Online prescription orders | Listed as an alternative optician channel [R 4.2.3] |
| 4 | **Fielmann / Apollo** | National scale | Fielmann says it is open to any manufacturer and has unused store capacity [R 4.2.3]. Both are named in the HateAid complaint [R 1.4], so **a camera-free product directly lowers their legal and reputational exposure.** That is our pitch to them. |
| 5 | **MediaMarkt / Saturn** | Mass consumer electronics retail | Later phase. They dominate CE retail, but shelf space is limited and they are also named in the HateAid complaint [R 4.1.8]. The same camera-free pitch applies. |

**Marketing and content (low budget, high credibility):**
- **Privacy-by-design narrative tied to the public debate.** Our product is a concrete example of the "technical solution" Bitkom calls for [R 4.2.1]. Pitch to German tech media (heise, t3n, netzwelt, mixed: the outlets cited in the report) and offer test units.
- **Demonstration content:** live captions in a conversation, translated subtitles while travelling, and a side-by-side comparison of "what a bystander sees" (no camera lens).
- **Launch event in a major German city** (Rokid used Munich).
- **Trade fair presence: IFA** (Even G2 won an IFA 2026 award [R 2.2], showing the category gets attention there). Optician trade fairs could matter for channel recruitment, but the report does not name any: **insufficient information, further research recommended.**

### 4.2 Phased plan

**Timing constraint:** The report sees a window from 2026 to H1 2027 before Google/Samsung and Apple arrive [R 4.2.8]. Today is 18 Nov 2026, so most of that window has already passed. The EU Battery Regulation changes from Feb 2027 [R 4.1.5]. Realistically we can catch the end of the window only if product and compliance are already far along. **Our product readiness is not covered by the report: insufficient information, further research recommended.** The phases below are sequenced by gates, and the dates are indicative.

**Phase 0 – Validate and comply (Dec 2026 – Q1 2027)**
- Quantitative survey of German glasses wearers: interest in camera-free display glasses, willingness to pay in the €500-600 range, and the value of prescription included. This closes the main sizing gap.
- Hands-on benchmark against Even G2, Rokid Glasses and Meta Ray-Ban Display on German-language AI, captions, latency, comfort and battery.
- Legal opinion (German counsel) on Battery Regulation applicability, microphone/§ 201 StGB design, AI Act/GPAI provider compliance and data-protection documentation. Start CE/RED/EN 18031 testing.
- Recruit 10-20 urban independent opticians as pilot partners. This number is a planning assumption, not a figure from the report.
- **Gate:** the survey confirms willingness to pay, the compliance path is clear, and unit economics at €500-600 work.

**Phase 1 – Pilot launch in large cities (Q2 2027)**
- Sell through pilot opticians plus the D2C shop, Amazon.de (non-prescription) and Mister Spex.
- Launch event, media test units and privacy-by-design content campaign.
- Measure optician sell-through, return rate, prescription share and NPS. Set target values after Phase 0; the report gives no baseline.
- **Gate:** sell-through and return rate show product-market fit for segment 1.

**Phase 2 – Scale the channel (H2 2027)**
- Approach Fielmann and Apollo with pilot data plus the camera-free compliance argument (lower HateAid-type exposure).
- Expand to more independent opticians beyond the pilot cities.
- Consider a MediaMarkt/Saturn listing for the non-prescription variant.
- Have a defence plan ready for possible Android XR / Apple EU launches: lean on German localisation, prescription and the privacy story. Do not get drawn into a price war.

**Phase 3 – Adjacent segments (2028+)**
- **Accessibility (hard-of-hearing captions):** only after verifying the reimbursement route with health insurers and aid bodies [R 3.1, segment 5, unverified].
- **Enterprise:** evaluate a separate line for HoloLens 2 replacement accounts (security updates end 31 Dec 2027 [R 2.3]), possibly via TeamViewer Frontline or SAP-integrated ISVs [R 4.2.5]. This needs ruggedisation, a works-council-compatible design and enterprise sales capability. Feasibility is **insufficient information, further research recommended.**
- Other DACH markets (Austria, Switzerland): the report covers Germany only, so **insufficient information, further research recommended.**

### 4.3 Key risks to the strategy

| Risk | Source | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| The German market is a niche with demand below the European average | Fielmann CEO: "closer to sports sunglasses than AirPods" [R 1.3] | Phase 0 validation gate. Start small via pilot opticians rather than a national rollout. |
| Even G2 cuts prices or strengthens its German AI | Closest competitor [R 2.2] | Compete on German-first AI quality and the EU data promise, not only price. |
| Platform giants arrive in 2027 | [R 2.2, 4.2.8] | Lock in optician partnerships early. Localisation and prescription fitting are harder to copy quickly than features (inference). |
| Camera-free limits appeal for some buyers | The 43% interest in the Bitkom survey covers smart glasses in general, not camera-free ones [R 1.3] | Concede the creator segment. Validate camera-free acceptance in the Phase 0 survey. |
| Compliance delays (battery, RED cybersecurity) | [R 4.1.5] | Start testing in Phase 0 and treat it as a launch gate. |
